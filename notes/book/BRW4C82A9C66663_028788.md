## COMMAND CODES

**CC SFDISP to check CC's that are assigned to your profile**

- **ENMOD** - displays the most current entity information for a TP
  - including pending charges, notices, and letters sent
- **INOLES** - Master file entity info/valid name checks with social security/cross reference spouse info
  - can be used for disclosure purposes.
- **TRDBV** - researches filed returns; both electronic and paper.
  - will show both accepted and rejected returns.
  - only lines TP filled out are reflected.
  - when the tax period is not used it will list every return currently accessible.
- **RTVUE** - displays original 1040 info that shows every line
  - will list all math errors reflected vs TXMOD only reflecting one at a time
- **TXMODA** - all tax module information for a specific tax period on the TIF.
  - stores the TIN, File Source, TIN Type, MFT, Tax Period, Case Control Indicator, Name Control and, for EPMF, Plan Number as required data elements for subsequent processing.
- **DDBCK** - used for validating additional children and credits claimed on amended returns.
  - displays the dependents & qualifying children from the original posted return or as last modified.
  - by entering update request into DDBCK, a user can then modify the data to reflect changes from the submitted 1040X.
- **IRPTR** - wage, income, and investment information
- **NAMES**: used to search the national file for a TP's return not located under their TIN.
- **INOLE**: displays the entity information as compared to the SSA or IRS Master File.
- **IMFOLE**: displays the posted entity information.
- **CFINK**: searches the CAF for the entity information on the taxpayer's authorized representative.
- **ESTAB**: requests a paper processed return from files.
- **FFINQ**: shows when the return has been input and the current status of the return.
  - used especially when disclosing a first-time filer.

Statute searching a case:
- See if the case in question is a statute year
- Look at the ASED (if more than 90 days out, no need to continue. If 90 days or less continue!)
- Determine how much tax is currently on the account
  - Take the TC150 amount and add/subtract and amounts from any adjustments i.e. TC's 29X/30X/40X
- Look at the taxpayer's claim and determine whether they are making any changes to their total tax
  - Line 11, column C on the Form 1040X
  - **If the number is increasing send the claim to Statutes**
- Look at the taxpayer's claim and determine if they are making any changes to their credits
  - Line 12-15, column C, the payment section of the Form 1040X
  - **If the number for their credits is decreasing in comparison to what is on TXMODA, send the claim to Statutes**

**Do not follow these procedures if the case is a tax increase (or credit decrease) and the ASED is within 5 days! - See IRM 25.6.1.9.9.2 & your lead/manager immediately.**

1. Only cases that are process as original can be sent to statute through CIS under suspend. They can stamp the case on CIS before you submit it for processing. All other cases must be hand carried.
2. If the case is a tax increase (or credit decrease) and the ASED is within 90 days do the following:
3. Print the CIS case (only the first 10 pages are needed or pages most relevant to the case).
4. Print TXMODA, IMFOLT, and ENMOD.
5. Complete Form 10959 (it is best to complete it on the screen and print 3 copies).
6. Attach the 3 copies of Form 10959, CIS case, and IDRS prints.
7. Suspend your CIS case for 1 week using monitor.
8. Update your activity on IDRS using:
   - `ACTON`
   - `C0?,STATUXX-XX,S` (? Will depend on which case control is yours, XX-XX is the date one week from the date of input)
9. Give the paperwork to your designated statute runner or walk case to Statutes.
10. If your case is not returned within 1 week, follow up with your statute runner.
11. If the case is returned with "KEPT" written on the Form 10959 then leave a CASE NOTE (not case message) "KEPT BY STATUTE" on CIS. Input a TC 971-014. Close your CIS case.
12. If the case is returned with other instructions follow those instructions.

- **ASED** - tax increase/credit decrease assessment expiration date **within 90 days get it out to statute department so it's not barred**
- **180 days or less forward any reinput return within 180 days of the ASED to the Statute team for clearance prior to routing to any other area.**
- **RSED** - refund statute expiration 3 years
- **CSED** - collection expiration date 10 years
- **ERSED** - erroneous incorrect refund statue expiration 2 years from issuance date if IRS error or inadvertent TP error/ 5 years from refund issuance date if TP error by fraud

**Expired:**
If there is no reply or the request for additional information is received after the requested response date and the RSED has expired, disallow the claim per IRM 21.5.3.4.3(5), Tax Decrease and Statute Consideration:
- **Procedures:**
  - Send certified Letter 105C and include appeal rights
  - Input TC 290 for .00 with the appropriate reason code and blocking series 98 or 99
  - If the original return was filed electronically, use blocking series 99 and attach TRPRT or the IMFOLR/BMFOLR print

**Not expired:**
If there is no reply received by the requested response date and the RSED has not expired, follow the "No Consideration" procedures as outlined in IRM 21.5.3.4.6.3, No Consideration
- **Procedures:**
  - Send Letter 916C to include why the claim is not being considered
  - Input TC 290 for .00 with appropriate reason code and blocking series
  - DO NOT use blocking series 98 or 99

**More than 90 days ASED**
If more than 90 days remain on the ASED, and Form 1040X is a tax increase or credit decrease, input the adjustment.

**90 days or less ASED**
If 90 days or less remain on the ASED, and Form 1040X requires a tax increase or credit decrease, take no action on the account and the functional manager must hand carry to Statutes per IRM 25.6.1.9.9.2, After Hours and Imminent Assessments.

## Unpostable Conditions

- ***UPC 126-0 Gets treated differently***
- ***25.25.6.6 is the mother IRM for all things related to Unpostable condition 126-0.***
- 21.4.1.4.1.2.1 Unpostable Conditions
- 21.5.5.3 Unpostables Research
- 21.5.5.4.2.2 Unpostables Created by Your Own Adjustments
- 21.5.5.3.3 Responding to Taxpayer Inquiries on an Open Unpostable
- 21.5.5.3.4 Responding to Taxpayer Inquiries on a Closed Unpostable
- TCD 0070 Unpostables: Research and Resolution

A return that was received but was unable to complete processing/an adjustment, credit transfer, or entity change that is not accepted by Master File.
- research unpostables using CC's TXMODA & ENMOD.
- the RESULTS tool from the IAT tool bar will give you the most up to date information vs going straight to CC UPTIN
- If you didn't cause unpostable at the most leave notes and give TP timeframe. When responding to TP determine if unpostable is open or closed look at timeframe less or more than 8 weeks. Go to IRM to see what to tell TP
- look for the status, MFT, tax period, what cycle and date

**Open Unpostable**
- a transaction on the account did not post due to other factors/errors within the account
- generally handled within eight cycles
- timeframe for resolution can be extended depending on the complexity of the unpostable

**Closed Unpostable**
- someone took the appropriate action needed to close the condition that was previously causing an error
- use the status date of the closed unpostable to determine the appropriate timeframe to respond to taxpayer inquires

**How to solve unpostables:**
1. Identifying the Unpostable
   - (TXMODA/up history, results tool) the cause, what number is it?
2. Researching the Unpostable
   - (what's the status open or close and the date, what number)
3. Resolving the Unpostable
   - (go to timeframes job aid/IRM to see what to tell the TP)
4. Responding to the Taxpayer
   - Give the TP the appropriate timeframe from the IRM

**Common unpostable reasons:**
- Dates or money amounts do not match on credit transfers
- Adjustments are input to an account with no transaction code (TC) 150 posted
- Priority codes (PCs) are omitted
- Amounts are reduced below zero, except adjusted gross income (AGI)
- Errors are made in data entry
- Payments are input on settled accounts.
- Freeze codes are not considered
- Pending transactions are not considered
- Presence of certain identity theft indicators

**Unpostables someone else caused:**
- When responding to TP determine if unpostable is open or closed look at timeframe less or more than 8 weeks. Go to IRM to see what to tell TP

**Unpostable YOU caused:**
- Category code "NLUN" = employee created unpostable
- What did you do to cause it?
- Is it something you input? If that is the case the unpostable must DQ in order for you to attempt the adjustment again.

**When a claim is disallowed it is done so because:**
- the issue being considered is not allowable by law
- the issue was not filed timely-unless they have a time machine, nothing can change this.
- only disallow claims once complete research and all contacts have been made for any missing information

**When a claim is partially disallowed it is done so because:**
- the issue being considered is in part not allowable by law, but another portion of the claim is allowable

**When a claim is not considered it is because:**
- the taxpayer did not provide enough documentation to qualify for their claim
- the taxpayer did not respond to a request for missing information

## Payment Tracing

- 21.5.7.3 Missing Payments Research

Questions to ask the taxpayer:
- When did you send the payment in?
- How much is the payment for?
- How did you make the payment? Electronically or through the postal mail?
- What kind of payment is this? *i.e. settling liability, estimated tax payment, remittance with a return*
  - **By mail –**
    - If payment was put in the mail and it's been less than 2 weeks, ask the taxpayer to allow a minimum of two weeks for it to post and then call back to verify receipt.
    - If the payment was made more than 2 weeks do further research.
  - **Electronically –**
    - It has been less than one week, tell the taxpayer to allow a minimum of one week for it to post and then call back to verify receipt.
    - If the payment was made more than 1 week prior do further research.

**Research (TC Search tool on IAT)**
- What type of payment?
- What is the date and amount of payment?
- Was the payment split and it what amounts?

**Types of payments**
- **Payments received with tax return** will have a different DLN then the return
- **Payments received in a Taxpayer Assistance Center (TAC)** - These payments have a 13-digit remittance sequence number
- **Lockbox payment** - TP's mail payments to lockboxes at banks
- **Electronic payments** - payments can be made online by credit card, and through Direct Debit Installment Agreements (DDIAs)
- **Multiple Remittance** - when two or more payments are received with one document **"M"** notated in the upper left margin of the document. The back of the processed check or money order is also notated to indicate the multiple remittance.
- **Split Remittance** - payments that should be applied to more than one return document, or to more than one tax period. An **"S"** is notated in the upper left margin of the document. The back of the processed check or money order is notated to indicate the split remittance.
- **Encoding Error** - an encoding error that occurs when a check is encoded with a different amount than the check was originally written for
- **Piggyback checks** - are two attached checks that are run through the encoding machine as one

**Remittance Transaction Register (RTR) Research**
- RTR is on AMS
- Stores check records up to 7 years

**Research payments with:**
- DLN
- Trace id
- Tax Period
- Name control
  - You **don't have the DLN or Trace ID? Deposit date and site code are needed.**
- **Wildcards (*)** are put into fields when you don't know the full information for the fields.

**Results of payment search will show**
- Deposit date / -Transaction date
- MFT / -DLN
- TIN / -Tax Period
- Name Control / -Transaction Code
- Amount
- Routing Number

## Balance Due/Penalties

Disclaimer:
You are **NOT** balance due trained or certified, the following information is for **knowledge purposes only**. **DO NOT** get into conversations with taxpayers over their balances, penalties or their installment agreements. Your scope in response to any balance due issues pertains to the following:
- verify payments made on the account
  - the amount
  - the date
  - what the payment is in reference to
- run the RCA tool if a tp requests an abatement of penalties
- more often than not, getting them to the balance due/ Collections department

- 5.1.15.16.1 Reasonable Cause Assistant (RCA)
- TCD 0081 FTF
- TCD 0082 FTP
- TCD 0083 Estimated Tax Penalty
- TCD 0085 Bad check

**CP Notices = Unpaid Balance Due**
- **XX*** - 5 weeks until next notice, collection status 19/21, Notice issued from Master File when a return or adjustment posts
- **501** - 5 weeks until next notice, collection status 20, Second notice issued on below deferral accounts only
- **503** - 5 weeks until next notice, collection status 56, Second notice on accounts above deferral, or third notice on below deferral accounts
- **504** - 5 weeks until next notice, collection status 58, Final notice (certified)

**FTF - Failure to file**
- 5% if the unpaid tax for each month, or part of the month the return is late for.

**FTP - Failure to pay**
- Tax on original return is filed without being fully paid by the due date.

**When a return with a balance due is filed and/or the tax is paid after the due date, it is subject to Failure to File (FTF) and Failure to Pay (FTP) penalties.**

**Order in which payments are applied to their unpaid balance due**
- Unpaid tax
- Penalties
- Interest

Under $100,000 you have 21 days before penalties start accruing from the day of the notice, $100,000 or more you only have 10 days from the day of the notice

**Estimated Tax Penalty**
- The lesser of 90% of current year's tax or 100% of previous year's tax whichever is less
- **ES Payment due date**
  - The installment due dates for calendar year taxpayers are
    - April 15th
    - June 15th
    - September 15th
    - January 15th of the following year

**Form4868**
- extension to file (6-month extension)
- an extension to file is NOT AN EXTENSION TO PAY.

**Reasonable Cause Assistant (RCA) is for FTF and FTP.**
- FTF and FTP are the only penalties to give relief for and abate with the RCA tool.
- Using the generated questions within the tool, determination is made on whether the penalties can be abated.
- Oral statement authority ceiling is a threshold of how much money we can abate over the phone, if they request more than the threshold, they must write in.
  - $1,000 per penalty and/or $2,000 per module if RCA is used.
- First Time Penalty Abatement equates to every taxpayer getting a "freebie." If they qualify for this kind of abatement, then a window will immediately populate saying that they qualify.
- RCA can also make the determination that the penalty will be "Sustained" meaning they do not qualify for their penalties to be abated over the phone. The taxpayer can always submit Form 843.

***Penalties and interest continue to add up on an account as long as a liability remains on the account.***

## Call Flow/ Tips

| The Six Step Process | Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1. Greet the taxpayer | - Identify yourself<br>- Indicate willingness to help |
| 2. Respond to the taxpayer's opening statement | - Actively listen to taxpayer<br>- Deal with taxpayer's feelings, if appropriate<br>- Acknowledge that you will help |
| 3. Target the taxpayer's question | - Inquire and paraphrase the issue back to the taxpayer<br>- Gain agreement from taxpayer |
| 4. Get the necessary facts | - Ask the right questions to get the information necessary to answer the inquiry<br>- Use purpose statements prior to gathering information from the taxpayer |
| 5. Provide assistance | - Provide accurate and complete information<br>- Explain any procedures<br>- Order necessary forms/publications<br>- Appropriately refer taxpayer to another resource, if required |
| 6. Close the conversation | - Verify taxpayer's understanding<br>- Conclude contact courteously<br>- Apologize if IRS made an error |
